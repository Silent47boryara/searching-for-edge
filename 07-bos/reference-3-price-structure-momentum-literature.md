# Reference III — Price Structure, Path Continuity & Momentum-Turning-Point Literature

Literature reviewed for [Note 07 (BOS)](bos-a-structural-scanner-not-a-predictor.md) — a structural break/impulse detection system, not a cross-sectional factor strategy. The research question here is narrower than Reference I: not "is there a tradeable factor," but "does the internal shape of a price path, or an observable disagreement between fast/slow signals, carry information about the birth, persistence, or end of an individual trend episode." Status tags follow this project's own screening verdicts, carried over as-is: `DEEP DIVE` (directly relevant to trend/path lifecycle, read in full as an engineering spec) · `SUPPORTING` (relevant background or a bounding result, not a lifecycle model itself) · `SKIP` (read, judged not relevant to this specific question) · `Queued` (identified, not yet screened).

## Price Path Shape & Continuity

- Da, Z., Gurun, U. G., Warachka, M. (2014). "Frog in the Pan: Continuous Information and Momentum." *The Review of Financial Studies*, 27(7). U.S. equities, 1976–2007 (extended sample from 1927). Introduces Information Discreteness (ID): at the same cumulative formation-period return, a continuous price path (many small same-direction days) produces materially stronger and longer momentum (~8 months) than a discrete path (~2 months). Foundational result for the idea that path shape matters beyond magnitude; does not define individual trend formation or a real-time termination rule — a fixed 12-month cross-sectional window. `DEEP DIVE`
- Kim, S. (2026). "Price Path Continuity and the Cross-Section of Cryptocurrency Returns." SSRN, 2026 preprint (not peer-reviewed). Broad crypto panel, Jan 2020–Apr 2026, survivorship-bias-mitigated via historical CoinMarketCap snapshots (~1,293 → ~2,956 coins). Crypto-native follow-up to Da/Gurun/Warachka: a 14-day Rank-Weighted Price Path Continuity (PPC) measure interacts positively and significantly with past return; winner-minus-loser spread nearly vanishes among discrete-path coins, becomes large (~1.16%/week diff-in-diff) among continuous-path coins. Robust across alternative formation/holding windows, market states, and volume regimes; value-weighted effect weaker due to mega-cap dominance. Cross-sectional, not a single-asset time-series or real-time episode model. Closest published match to this project's own open path-continuity result. `DEEP DIVE`
- Borgards, O. (2021). "Dynamic Time Series Momentum of Cryptocurrencies." 20 cryptocurrencies on Bitfinex, mostly 2014–2019, plus S&P 500 and simulated GBM as controls; 1D/1H/5m frequencies. Defines momentum dynamically via turning points: a first directional price cycle (formation) is often followed by further same-direction cycles (continuation), ending when the higher-high/higher-low (or mirrored) sequence breaks. Crypto continuation is far stronger than the S&P 500 benchmark across tested frequencies; probability of a further continuation cycle declines as more cycles pass — momentum is not modeled as an infinitely stable state. Turning points depend on a smoothing-filter sensitivity parameter (κ=5) — "no arbitrary threshold" does not mean "unparameterized." Directly analogous to this project's own impulse state machine (§2 of Note 07). `DEEP DIVE`

## Turning Points & Observable Deterioration

- Goulding, C., Harvey, C. R., Mazzoleni, I. "Momentum Turning Points." SSRN. U.S. equity markets, 1969–2018 (plus a longer robustness history), plus 20 international equity markets, monthly. Defines an established trend as agreement between a slow (12-month) and fast (1-month) trailing-return signal; a turning point is disagreement (Correction = slow+/fast−, Rebound = slow−/fast+). Explicit that the true trend/turning-point state is latent — disagreement is only an observable proxy, not proof a reversal actually happened. Closest published analogue to this project's own impulse-ends logic. `DEEP DIVE`
- Goulding, C., Harvey, C. R., Mazzoleni, I. "Breaking Bad Trends." SSRN. 43 futures markets (11 equity indices, 8 government-bond markets, 24 commodities), monthly, 1990–2022. Companion result to the paper above: the frequency of slow/fast disagreement rose after the 2009 financial crisis, and static trend-following performance worsened alongside it. States explicitly that an observed turning point does not necessarily reflect an actual trend break — it can be a false alarm in a noisy period. Together, the pair shows a real observable deterioration state exists, but the authors themselves leave "temporary correction vs. real end of trend" unresolved — the same open question this project's own path-continuity work (§7 of Note 07) is circling from BTC-specific data. `DEEP DIVE`

## Is Momentum/Continuation a Stable Phenomenon, or Something Narrower?

- Liu, Y., Tsyvinski, A. (2018/2021). "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency." NBER Working Paper 25882, August 2018; *The Review of Financial Studies*, 34(6), 2021. Bitcoin (2011–May 2018), Ripple (2013–2018), Ethereum (2015–2018), CoinDesk prices; daily and weekly. Foundational time-series (not cross-sectional) crypto momentum result: a coin's own current return predicts its own future return at 1–7 day and 1–4 week horizons, strongest around 1–3 weeks; survives removing 2011–2012 with reduced magnitude, and a no-lookahead quintile design. Explicit limitations: irregular across horizons (non-monotonic day-to-day), several coefficients lose significance under bootstrap, R² typically 0–5%, no turning-point detector, no path-dependence analysis, no net-of-cost trading system. Investor attention (Google/Twitter search) is a separately significant predictor, distinct from realized volatility (which shows little predictive power). Foundational context for this project's own (rejected) direction-prediction attempt. `SUPPORTING`
- Dobrynskaya, V. (2021/2023). "Cryptocurrency Momentum and Reversal." *Journal of Alternative Investments*, 26(1), 65–76 (HSE University; first circulated 2021). ~2,000 coins, CoinMarketCap, 2014–2020, cross-sectional J/K design. Momentum is strongest at very short J/K (≲2 weeks, e.g. 2/2 ≈70% annualized); the sign flips to a significant, growing reversal beyond ~4–6 weeks, driven mainly by past losers. Explicit that short-horizon momentum weakened in later years and the result may not hold on smaller universes/subperiods. No survivorship/delisting treatment, sample ends before Terra/Luna and FTX. Cautions directly against treating "crypto has momentum" as a single stable fact independent of horizon. `SUPPORTING`
- Grobys, K., Kolari, J., Sandretto, R., Shahzad, S., Äijö, J. (2025). "Cryptocurrency Momentum Has (Not) Its Moments." *Financial Markets and Portfolio Management* (Springer). Top-30 crypto by market cap, equal-weighted, 1-month formation / 1-week holding, weekly rebalance, 2016–2023. Result is highly sample- and tail-sensitive: +1.74%/week (weak significance) in 2016–Jul 2020, −0.19%/week (insignificant) after, +0.90%/week (insignificant) full sample; a single idiosyncratic coin crash in Dec 2020 (one observation, 0.24% of the sample) accounts for ~37% of the full-sample return swing; estimated power-law tail exponent implies undefined variance. Strong anti-cherry-picking evidence that plain cross-sectional crypto momentum depends heavily on sample endpoint, universe, and rare extreme events. `SUPPORTING`

## Volume, Deterioration Heterogeneity & Risk Overlays (cross-referenced with Note 06)

- Gervais, S., Kaniel, R., Mingelgrin, D. (2001). "The High-Volume Return Premium." *The Journal of Finance*, 56(3). U.S. equities (NYSE/AMEX). Abnormally high-volume stocks outperform afterward, abnormally low-volume stocks underperform; the effect strengthens, not weakens, when contemporaneously extreme-return observations are excluded — volume is treated as information orthogonal to past-return autocorrelation, plausibly via visibility/attention. Cross-sectional stock effect over days/weeks, not a lifecycle model of an individual trend. Already cited in Note 06; cited again here as background for why volume/leverage data collection (§9 of Note 07) is worth doing at all. `SUPPORTING`
- Garcia Seuma, R. M. (2026). "Where Does the Criticality Live? Early-Warning Signals Are Event-Heterogeneous Across Seven Crypto-Perpetual Liquidation Cascades." arXiv:2607.27070. 7 major BTC perpetual liquidation cascades, 2022–2025, minute price + 5-minute leverage/order-flow data, 39 analysis configurations per variable/event. No single early-warning variable precedes all seven cascades: price shows the expected critical-slowing-down signature in 5 of 7 events but is silent ahead of two sudden-news shocks; the one cross-event regularity (compression in taker order-flow variance) holds at the population level (Fisher p≈5×10⁻⁶) but is not a reliable per-event alarm. Direct caution against assuming this project's own forced-flow/liquidation data will yield a clean, universal deterioration signal. Already cited in Note 06. `SUPPORTING`
- Han, Y., Zhou, G., Zhu, Y. (2016). "Taming Momentum Crashes: A Simple Stop-Loss Strategy." SSRN. U.S. equities, 1926–2013, standard 6-month/1-month cross-sectional momentum. A 15% stop-loss overlay cuts the worst monthly loss from −49.79% to −17.43% (equal-weighted) and more than doubles the Sharpe ratio. Already cited in Note 06 for its exit-management relevance; not screened as a lifecycle/deterioration model for Note 07 — it reacts to an already-realized drawdown rather than detecting deterioration in progress. `SKIP` for this note's specific question (relevant to execution design, not to BOS/path-structure detection)
